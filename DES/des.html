How does DES Key Schedule work?

64-bit Main Key: Contains 8 parity bits, leaving 56 effective key bits.
Permuted Choice 1 (PC-1): Drops parity bits and splits the key into two 28-bit halves (C & D).
Key Schedule: Left-shifts C & D by 1 or 2 bits across 16 rounds, then passes through PC-2 to produce sixteen 48-bit subkeys (K1 to K16).

What happens in Initial Permutation (IP)?

Before Feistel round processing begins, the 64-bit plaintext is rearranged according to a fixed IP table.
It splits the block into L₀ (32 bits) and R₀ (32 bits). After 16 Feistel rounds, IP⁻¹ (Inverse IP) undoes this initial permutation.
