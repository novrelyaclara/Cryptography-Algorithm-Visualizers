STEP 1

STEP 1: Input Message Plaintext & Pad Bits

SHA-256 requires the message plaintext to be padded so its bit length is congruent to 448 mod 512, ending with a 64-bit big-endian integer of the original message length.

Why do we pad the message plaintext?

512-bit Block Alignment: SHA-256 operates on fixed 512-bit plaintext blocks.
Padding Structure: Appends a single bit '1', followed by '0's, and finishes with a 64-bit integer representing the original plaintext length in bits.
This guarantees unique output hashes and prevents length extension attacks.

STEP 2

STEP 2: Prepare Message Schedule (W0 to W63)

Expand 16 words (32 bits each) from the plaintext block into 64 words using bitwise right rotations, bit shifts, and XOR operations.

How are words expanded from plaintext?

First 16 words W[0..15] come directly from the padded 512-bit plaintext block.
Words W[16..63] are calculated using: W[i] = σ1(W[i-2]) + W[i-7] + σ0(W[i-15]) + W[i-16].
Where σ0 and σ1 utilize ROTR (Right Rotate) and SHR (Shift Right) bitwise logic.
