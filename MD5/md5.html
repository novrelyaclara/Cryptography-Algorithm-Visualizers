STEP 1

STEP 1: Input Message Plaintext & Pad Bits

MD5 requires the message plaintext to be padded so its bit length is congruent to 448 mod 512, ending with a 64-bit little-endian integer of the original message length.

Why do we pad the message plaintext?

512-bit Block Alignment: MD5 operates on fixed 512-bit plaintext blocks.
Padding Structure: Appends a single bit '1', followed by '0's, and finishes with a 64-bit little-endian integer representing the original plaintext length in bits.
This guarantees unique output hashes and conditions the message for 32-bit word processing.

STEP 2

STEP 2: Prepare Message Schedule (M0 to M15)

Break the 512-bit padded plaintext block into 16 32-bit little-endian words (M0 to M15) used directly throughout MD5 compression rounds.

How are words prepared from plaintext?

The 16 words M[0..15] are derived directly from the padded 512-bit plaintext block.
Each word consists of 4 bytes arranged in little-endian order.
During the 64 compression steps, these 16 words are permuted in fixed order across 4 distinct rounds.
